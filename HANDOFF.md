## How the project actually unfolded

The project pivoted twice before the current shape emerged.

1. **Enforcement Tracker first (nb01).** The CMS GDPR Enforcement Tracker was scraped
   (3,195 rows) before realizing it carries no decision texts — fatal for text-based
   RQs. Kept because it is the only source with a `sector` field (see sector join, below).
2. **US pivot (nb02–nb03).** The FTC Legal Library "Privacy and Security" filter yields
   529 records, of which 339 are actual case nodes and 337 survive to the corpus.
   Press releases serve as decision-text surrogates (333 of 337). Naive penalty
   extraction was contaminated by statutory boilerplate ("civil penalty of up to $16,000
   per violation") in roughly 150 cases; the keyword-proximity extractor in nb03 fixes
   this, yielding 147 genuine penalties.
3. **GDPRhub rediscovered (nb06).** Initially written off because its frontend blocks
   scrapers, until we found its MediaWiki API. 2,417 DPA decisions with community-written
   Facts/Holding summaries — these are the EU decision-text surrogates.

Labels: 274 of 337 US cases take labels from Rafal Fryc's FTC Enforcement Provisions
Library, validated against the FTC's own topic tags (agreement 97.8–98.9% across
COPPA/FCRA/GLBA; 17 disagreement cases hand-audited, 5 corrections). **Note:** Rafal's
GitHub has since gone private, so his dataset is no longer independently retrievable —
our own scrape and the `label_source` column are what make the corpus self-sufficient.

## Decisions and why

- **K-means severity tiers instead of fine regression.** Orlando & Santoro (2025) show
  raw fine amounts conflate entity size and regulatory discretion with severity. Also
  log(0) is undefined, and no-fine outcomes are a different instrument (injunctions,
  compliance orders), hence the separate No Fine tier.
- **Surrogate decision texts.** True GDPR decision texts are long (well past Legal-BERT's
  512 tokens), often untranslated, and unevenly available. Community summaries and press
  releases are tractable and consistent; acknowledged in the paper's Limitations.
- **Coarse 4-class taxonomy with a priority rule.** Article 5 is cited so broadly it
  would swallow everything; the mapping checks Security articles (32, 25), then Consent
  (6, 7, 8, 17), then Transparency (13, 14), else Other. US: COPPA and Privacy Shield →
  Consent, GLBA and HBNR → Security, Section 5-only → Transparency, FCRA and rest → Other.
- **Fixed mid-2026 FX rates** applied retroactively to 2018–2026 fines. A simplification,
  stated in Limitations; a year-matched conversion would be an easy improvement.

## Traps for the next person

- **nb09 rerun strips nb11's columns.** nb09 writes `unified_cases.csv` with only the 11
  base fields. `penalty_usd`, `severity_tier`, `coarse_label` come from nb11. Rerun order
  matters: nb09 → nb11 → nb12.
- **nb11's save cell sits before its taxonomy cell.** `coarse_label` is only in the CSV
  because the save was manually re-executed. A top-to-bottom rerun silently drops it.
- **nb04 looks dead but is not.** Its plots are superseded, but its labeling cells
  produce `ftc_labeled.csv`, which nb08 needs.
- **Seed inconsistency in nb12.** The Task 2 in-distribution cell prints macro F1 0.52
  (seed 777); the paper's Table IX value of 0.55 comes from the Ruohonen cell's text-only
  condition (seed 42). Same corpus and model, different split; the gap lives in the
  25-support Low tier. Regenerate all paper numbers from one cell consistently.
- **Shared press releases.** Some cases share one press release and thus identical text
  and penalty (Facebook/Cambridge Analytica cluster, the two Epic Games cases) —
  potential label leakage for text models.
- **Absolute paths.** All notebooks use `/Users/nic/Documents/MM2/...` paths. Fixing
  file paths is the first thing a new environment requires.
- **Paper prose vs code.** On 2026-07-29 the Methodology's taxonomy description was
  found to misdescribe the nb11 mapping and was corrected. The code was always the
  ground truth for every number in the Results tables.
